Hiring your first engineers when you are not technical

06 August 2026 1 min read

The most expensive hire is not the one who costs the most. It is the one whose decisions you inherit for the next three years.

Ignore the stack

Candidates will tell you which frameworks they know. That matters far less than you think. What matters is whether they can explain a technical trade-off to you, in plain language, without making you feel stupid — because you will need that skill from them every week.

Ask for a decision, not a definition

Instead of asking what a database index is, describe a real problem from your product and ask how they would approach it. Then ask what they would give up in exchange. Engineers who cannot name a trade-off have not yet operated under real constraints.

Get one paid trial

A week of paid work on a small, real task tells you more than four interviews. You learn how they communicate when they are stuck, whether they ask before assuming, and whether the thing they hand over is finished or merely working.
